Uncertainties and sensitivities (headline)

  • The route factor is the largest single driver of distance (1.30 default; literature range 1.20–1.40).
  • Pipeline CAPEX is a single HyNet/Cadent project figure restated to 2026 and scaled to DN800. ±30% is an assumed uncertainty, not a sourced range.

  • Hub coordinates are approximate. Straight-line distance is between hub points, not terminals.
  • Screening only: no transient flow, line-pack, elevation, accidental release or let-down energy recovery (on hold).

Route

Pick a UK corridor, place your own terminals on the map, or type coordinates. Pipeline length = straight-line distance × route factor (default 1.30).

The route factor adds length for bends, deviations and detours around terrain and obstacles. It does not add vertical rise, river or road crossings, or special works. The literature range is 1.20–1.40 (arXiv 2505.01124, which cites Galimova et al., Timmerberg & Kaltschmitt, Linsel & Bertsch and Hampp et al.); 1.30 is the paper's standard value. These values are not UK-specific.

Uncertainties and sensitivities (route)

  • Route length = straight-line distance × route factor. The route factor covers bends, deviations and detours; it does not add vertical rise (elevation).
  • River, road and rail crossings and other special works are not costed separately. They would add CAPEX not in the model.
  • The route factor is a literature range, not a UK-specific measurement. A corridor survey would replace it.
  • Hub coordinates are approximate public locations.

Economics

Electricity default is the DESNZ Energy Trends Q4 2025 manufacturing price (16.8p/kWh, excluding CCL). Pipeline CAPEX default £3.98m/km at DN800 (2026 money) is HyNet/Cadent's £1.63m/km for 18–24 inch (2021 money, Cadent project report), restated with CPI ×1.243 and scaled to DN800 with exponent 1.6. Discount rate 7% is an investor-rate assumption (see Method). Compressor and threshold values are still placeholders in GBP: replace them with sourced values or project quotes before use.

Uncertainties and sensitivities (specification)

  • Hydraulics: roughness, gas temperature, heat transfer to ground, bends and fitting losses are assumptions or from one source each; their effect on cost is small at this flow (see table).
  • Pressures: source pressure is limited by MAOP, which depends on the grade and wall. The wall (16 mm) is an assumption; the required wall at 66.2 bar is 15.3 mm.
  • Delivery pressure: 39 bar absolute (38 barg), from the OAD Section I offtake limit. Its effect on the UK default is small because the line does not need compression.
  • Supply and losses: leakage (0.2%/1000 km) is not sourced. Utilisation (90%) is an assumption based on an 8,000 h/yr carrier study.
  • Economics: CAPEX, diameter exponent (1.6, assumed), WACC, OPEX (4%), electricity (£168/MWh, DESNZ Q4 2025) and asset life (30 years) each carry uncertainty; the table shows the cost range for each.

08

Method and limits

Each step is stated so it can be checked against the numbers above.

  • Flow. Source MW × (1 − routine leakage) ÷ LHV (119.96 MJ/kg) gives kg/h. Net annual delivery = kg/h × 8,760 h × utilisation.
  • Real gas. Density, Z, viscosity, cp, k and the Joule-Thomson coefficient come from a NIST table (CoolProp, 5–140 bar, −10 to 50 °C), interpolated at local pressure and temperature.
  • Line march (balance method). The line is marched in 10 km steps from the source pressure. At each step: Darcy-Weisbach friction (Colebrook) plus fitting losses K·ρv²/2 reduce pressure; the Joule-Thomson effect and heat exchange with the ground (overall coefficient U, ground temperature) set the temperature.
  • Compression. A compressor is added only where the pressure would fall below the minimum line pressure. It raises the gas to the source pressure and aftercools it to the inlet temperature. Isentropic power uses the real-gas cp and k at the suction state, divided by compressor efficiency.
  • Pressure limits. MAOP = 2·t·F·SMYS/OD, using the IGEM/TD/1 Supplement 2 design factor for hydrogen (X52 0.50, X60 0.43, X65 0.40, X70 0.37) and API 5L SMYS. The source pressure should not exceed the MAOP.
  • Hydraulic capacity. The flow at which the Darcy drop over one capacity section equals (source pressure − minimum pressure) × efficiency.
  • Legacy ratio method. Kept for comparison. One station per spacing interval, each imposing the ratio Pin/Pout regardless of friction. This is the method behind earlier results and overstates compression at this flow.
  • Cost. (Pipeline + compressor CAPEX) × CRF + fixed OPEX + compressor electricity, divided by net annual kg.
  • Leakage. Routine loss only, at the stated % per 1000 km. External accidental release is not modelled.
  • Interpretations. The pipeline efficiency factor multiplies the allowable pressure drop when computing hydraulic capacity. The 20 m/s (amber) and 30 m/s (red) velocity bands are screening choices, not a published standard. Discount rate is 7% nominal, an investor-rate assumption. For reference, Ofgem's final RIIO-3 gas WACC is 5.18% (semi-nominal, regulated network, low risk); no published UK hydrogen-pipeline investor hurdle rate was found, so 7% is a judgement to be replaced with a project's own rate. The capacity section length, leakage, OPEX and lifetime are still inherited defaults and are not independently sourced. The straight-line route length understates real routing; a 1.25 route factor raises the Teesside–London cost from £0.72 to £0.90/kg.
  • Scope. Dedicated H₂ pipelines only. Repurposed gas pipelines are not assumed equivalent and are not modelled here.
  • Defaults. Electricity is sourced (DESNZ, Q4 2025, manufacturing). Pipeline CAPEX is sourced (HyNet/Cadent, restated to 2026 and scaled to DN800). Ground temperature 10.6 °C is the BGS mean soil temperature (GR_999999/1, 2018). Design factor and SMYS are from IGEM/TD/1 Supplement 2 and API 5L. The overall heat-transfer coefficient (2 W/m²K), wall thickness (16 mm), bends per km and K per bend are assumptions; compressor and threshold values are placeholders in GBP.
  • Result sensitivity. Friction at this throughput drops pressure by well under 1 bar per 100 km, so compression depends mainly on the pressure window. With a 66.2 bar source and a 39 bar delivery pressure, the UK default (460 km pipeline with route factor 1.30) needs no compressor; the gas arrives at about 64 bar. Source and delivery pressure within the tested ranges therefore change the UK headline by zero (see the sensitivity table). Pressure let-down at delivery is on hold and not costed.

Uncertainties and sensitivities (full register)

  • Model form: steady state, Darcy-Weisbach with Colebrook friction, NIST real-gas properties, Joule-Thomson and ground heat exchange, one compressor type. Not modelled: transient flow, line-pack, elevation, impurities, fatigue, let-down energy recovery (on hold), accidental release, repurposed-pipe derating, compressor motor losses.
